The EU AI Act is the world's first broad law for artificial intelligence. It entered into force in 2024 with a staggered timetable, and the biggest block of obligations, the rules for "high-risk" AI systems, was due to start in August 2026.

That date has now moved. Here is what changed, what didn't, and why the delay isn't a reason to stop preparing.

## What happened

In 2025 the European Commission proposed a "Digital Omnibus", a package to simplify several digital laws, including the AI Act. After negotiations, it was adopted as **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, published in the EU's Official Journal on **24 July 2026** and in force from **27 July 2026**.

Its most important effect is on timing. The main reason given was practical: the technical standards and guidance companies need to comply with the high-risk rules weren't ready in time.

## The new dates

| Obligation | Original date | New date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High-risk AI listed in **Annex III** (uses like hiring, education, credit and essential services) | 2 August 2026 | **2 December 2027** |
| High-risk AI in products covered by **Annex I** (EU product-safety laws, such as machinery and medical devices) | 2 August 2027 | **2 August 2028** |

## What did *not* move

It's easy to read "delay" as "pause". That would be a mistake. Several parts of the Act already apply, and the Omnibus didn't change them:

- **Banned practices** have been prohibited since **2 February 2025**. These include social scoring, manipulative techniques that cause significant harm, exploiting people's vulnerabilities, untargeted scraping of facial images, and emotion recognition in workplaces and schools (with narrow exceptions).
- **AI literacy** has applied since **2 February 2025**. Organisations that provide or deploy AI must take steps to ensure their staff have sufficient understanding of the systems they work with.
- **General-purpose AI (GPAI) model rules** have applied since **2 August 2025**. These cover documentation, copyright policies and, for the most capable models, extra risk-management duties. They weren't delayed.
- **Most transparency obligations** keep their original timetable. For example, people must be told when they are interacting with an AI system, and certain AI-generated content must be marked as such.

## Why Annex III matters for so many teams

The Annex III list covers areas where AI decisions can seriously affect people's lives. It includes AI used in:

- **education and vocational training**, such as deciding admissions, assessing students or detecting cheating;
- **employment**, such as screening CVs, ranking candidates or evaluating employees;
- access to **essential services**, such as credit scoring or insurance pricing;
- **biometrics**, **critical infrastructure**, **law enforcement**, **migration** and the **administration of justice**.

Many ordinary business tools fall into these categories. A hiring platform that ranks applicants or an ed-tech product that grades students may well be high-risk. Providers of these systems will need risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy and cybersecurity controls, and a conformity assessment before they go on the market.

## What to do with the extra time

The new dates give teams roughly sixteen extra months for Annex III systems. Used well, that is enough time to do this properly rather than in a rush:

1. **Make an AI inventory.** List every AI system you build, buy or use, and what it is used for.
2. **Classify each one.** Is it prohibited, high-risk, subject to transparency duties, or minimal risk? Use the use case, not the technology, to decide.
3. **Close the gaps that already apply**, especially AI literacy and transparency notices.
4. **For likely high-risk systems, start the documentation now**: training data sources, testing results, known limits and human-oversight procedures.
5. **Watch the standards.** Harmonised European standards for high-risk AI are being finalised. They will turn the law's principles into concrete checklists.
6. **Talk to your suppliers.** If you use third-party models or AI services, find out what documentation and guarantees they will provide.

## It applies beyond Europe

Like the GDPR, the AI Act applies to companies outside the EU when they place AI systems on the EU market, or when their systems' outputs are used in the EU. Indian, American and other companies selling into Europe need to plan for it too.

## The takeaway

The Digital Omnibus bought time, not an exemption. Bans, AI literacy and general-purpose AI rules are already in force, and the high-risk rules are still coming, now in December 2027 and August 2028. Teams that use the extra months to inventory, classify and document their AI will be ready. Teams that treat it as a pause will be rushing again in 2027.
